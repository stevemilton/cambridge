Chapter 06

Storage & Freezing

The rules that keep batch cooking safe, and the freezer running like a second pantry.

The five non-negotiables

  1. Cool fast, chill fast. Get cooked food from hot to fridge-cold within two hours. Spread rice and stews in shallow containers to speed it up; never put steaming food straight into a packed fridge.
  2. The fridge runs at 0–5°C. Check it once with a fridge thermometer; most run warmer than their dial claims.
  3. Label everything with contents and date. Freezer bags flat-frozen stack like books and defrost in half the time.
  4. Defrost in the fridge overnight, never on the counter. A frozen dinner moves to the fridge the evening before its slot on the plan.
  5. Reheat once, to piping hot (steaming in the middle, 75°C+), and never refreeze anything that has been defrosted.

What not to freeze

Salad leaves, cucumber, raw tomatoes, cooked egg whites (they turn rubbery), yogurt-based sauces (they split), avocado for slicing, and anything already reheated once. Cooked pasta in sauce survives; plain cooked pasta goes gluey — freeze sauce and pasta separately if you can.

Reheating without ruining

  • Ragus, stews, soups: saucepan over medium heat with a splash of water, stirring, until bubbling — better texture than the microwave. Microwave works fine when time wins: cover, stir halfway, stand for a minute.
  • Rice: microwave with a tablespoon of water per portion, covered, until piping hot throughout. Once only.
  • Roasted vegetables and potatoes: 8–10 minutes in a 200°C oven or air fryer. The microwave turns them to sponge.
  • Koftas and burgers: 10–12 minutes at 180°C fan, covered in foil for the first half.
  • Fish: don’t. The plan never asks you to reheat plain cooked fish — where fish appears in leftovers it is eaten cold (Niçoise, sandwiches) or baked from raw in that day’s cooking.
