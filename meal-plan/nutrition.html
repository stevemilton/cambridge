Nutrition Principles

The numbers behind the plan — and the deliberately different rules for a growing fifteen-year-old.

Your daily targets

TargetAmountWhy
Energy≈ 2,000 kcal/dayA ~500 kcal daily deficit against estimated maintenance of ~2,500–2,600 kcal → roughly 0.5kg loss per week.
Protein150–170g/day (1.75–2.0g/kg)The strongest lever for preserving muscle in a deficit; also the most satiating macronutrient.
Carbohydrate≈ 180–220g/dayEnough to fuel Tuesday/Thursday lifting and tennis; placed strategically around training.
Fat≈ 60–70g/dayMostly olive oil, oily fish, avocado, nuts and seeds — the Mediterranean backbone.
Fibre30g+/daySatiety, gut health and heart health. Vegetables, beans, oats and wholegrains do this automatically.
Fluids2.5–3 litres/dayMore on training and tennis days. Thirst masquerades as hunger constantly during a diet.

The daily framework

The plan is built on three meals and two snacks. The recipes handle the meals; the snacks are yours to place, and they matter — they carry roughly 40g of the day’s protein.

Training-day timing (Tuesday & Thursday, 9–10am)

  • 7:30–8:00am — breakfast. The plan schedules carbohydrate-forward breakfasts on these days (Bircher oats, yogurt & oat bowl, salmon scramble on toast). Eat at least an hour before lifting.
  • 10:15am — post-training snack. Greek yogurt, honey and a banana. Protein plus fast carbohydrate, within an hour of finishing.
  • 1:00pm — lunch. Training-day lunches are the rice, couscous and grain-bowl recipes. This is where recovery actually happens.
  • Tennis days: treat the same way — carbs before, protein and fluids after. If a match runs long, add an extra banana; do not “bank” calories by skipping.

The rule that protects your lifts

Never train on a rest-day menu. If your training schedule moves, the carb-forward meals move with it. The plan serves the training, not the other way round.

Ten principles the recipes are built on

  1. Protein anchors every plate. 150–200g of lean protein raw weight per meal for you — chicken breast, fish, prawns, lean beef, turkey, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese.
  2. Olive oil is measured, not poured. It is health food at 1–2 tablespoons and a stealth 300 kcal at a glug. Every recipe specifies millilitres.
  3. Vegetables take half the plate — and they are roasted, charred, dressed and seasoned, because bland vegetables are how diets die.
  4. Carbohydrates are wholegrain or potato, in stated gram weights, scaled up for the teenager and on training days.
  5. Flavour is free. Lemon, garlic, chilli, smoked paprika, cumin, oregano, sumac, fresh herbs, vinegar, mustard: use them recklessly.
  6. Nothing ultra-processed. If it has an ingredient list you couldn’t write yourself, it’s not in the book.
  7. Sauces are yogurt-based, not cream-based — tzatziki, minted yogurt, lemon-dill yogurt — protein where mayonnaise used to be.
  8. Fish twice a week minimum, one of them oily (salmon). Standard UK heart-health guidance, and it makes the plan taste expensive.
  9. Weigh the calorie-dense, eyeball the rest. Oil, rice, pasta, oats, nuts and cheese get scales. Spinach does not need a barcode.
  10. One meal off-plan per week is part of the plan. A pizza night or a pub lunch inside an otherwise consistent week changes nothing except your sanity. Take it without guilt and without “making up for it”.

Her plan: the same table, different rules

Your daughter’s needs at fifteen are not a smaller version of yours — in many ways they are larger. Growth, sport and development put her energy needs at roughly 2,200–2,600+ kcal per day, her calcium needs higher than yours (around 1,000mg/day, roughly 3–4 servings of dairy), and her iron needs nearly double yours (around 15mg/day, which the red meat, dark greens and beans in this plan supply well).

Her standing adjustments

  • Carbohydrate portions are 1.5–2× yours. Every recipe states the exact adjustment — typically +75–100g cooked rice or pasta, +100–150g potatoes, or +1 pitta/wrap/slice of bread.
  • Full-fat dairy by default. Where you use 0% Greek yogurt, she can have full-fat; whole milk on cereal and in drinks.
  • Snacks are free and frequent. Toast with peanut butter, fruit, yogurt, milk, a bowl of cereal after training, cheese and crackers. These sit on top of the meals, not instead of them.
  • Dessert exists. Yogurt with honey and fruit, a square of chocolate, a weekend bake. Normal food for a normal teenager.
  • Match and training days: add an extra carbohydrate snack an hour or two before (toast and honey, a banana, fruit loaf) and milk or a yogurt drink afterwards.

What she should never see

Her plate being weighed. Her food being tracked. Calories discussed as good or bad. This book’s numbers exist for one adult’s training goal; for her, the plan is simply “we eat great food, and you get the big scoop of rice.”

Reading the recipe macro panels

Every recipe shows five numbers — kcal, protein, carbs, fat, fibre — per adult (fat-loss) serving, calculated from standard UK food composition data. Two honest caveats: nutrition data is an estimate wherever it appears (a ±5–10% margin is normal and fine), and your consistency matters a hundred times more than the second decimal place. Hit the framework, trust the trend.
