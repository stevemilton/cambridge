Breakfast 01 · Training-day favourite

Greek Yogurt & Berry Crunch Bowl with Warm Berry Compote & Toasted Oat-Almond Clusters

Thick, cold yogurt under a spoonful of warm, jammy berries and a handful of golden, honey-toasted clusters. The crunch is made in a weekly batch, so the morning itself takes four minutes.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 5 min (+15 min for the weekly crunch batch)
  • 🔥 Cook: 5 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (crunch batch serves 6)
420kcal
34gprotein
42gcarbs
13gfat
7gfibre

Per adult serving: 300g yogurt, 100g compote, 45g crunch.

Ingredients

The oat-almond crunch (weekly batch, 6 servings)

  • 150g porridge oats
  • 50g flaked almonds
  • 40g mixed pumpkin & sunflower seeds
  • 30g honey
  • 15ml light olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • Pinch fine sea salt

The compote (serves 2)

  • 200g frozen mixed berries
  • 10g honey
  • ½ lemon, juice only

To serve (per 2 people)

  • 300g 0% fat Greek yogurt (him)
  • 250g full-fat Greek yogurt (her)
  • 80g fresh berries

Equipment

  • Baking tray + baking paper
  • Small saucepan
  • Mixing bowl
  • Airtight jar for the crunch

Method

  1. Make the crunch (Sunday, 15 minutes). Heat the oven to 170°C fan. Toss the oats, almonds and seeds with the honey, oil, cinnamon and salt until everything glistens. Spread on a lined tray in a thin layer.
  2. Bake for 12–14 minutes, stirring once halfway, until deep golden and smelling like a bakery. It crisps as it cools — leave it completely cold on the tray, then break into clusters and jar. Keeps 2 weeks.
  3. On the morning: tip the frozen berries, honey and lemon juice into a small saucepan over medium heat. Simmer for 4–5 minutes, crushing a few berries against the side, until glossy and jammy but with whole berries remaining.
  4. Spoon the yogurt into two wide bowls. Swirl though nothing — leave it thick and pristine.
  5. Top with the warm compote, letting it bleed into the yogurt at the edges, then the fresh berries.
  6. Finish with the crunch clusters added at the very last second so they stay loud.

Chef’s tip

Warm compote + cold yogurt is the whole trick — don’t let the compote cool. And never stir the crunch in; it should crackle against the spoon.

Make ahead

Crunch: weekly batch. Compote: make a triple batch and fridge it — 30 seconds in the microwave brings it back to warm.

Storage

Crunch: airtight jar, 2 weeks. Compote: fridge, 4 days. Assembled bowls don’t store — assembly takes 90 seconds, so they don’t need to.

Freezing

Compote freezes for 3 months in ice-cube trays. The crunch doesn’t need the freezer and would soften there.

For your daughter

Full-fat yogurt, 60–70g of crunch (half as much again), and a sliced banana under the compote. On school-sport days, a slice of toast and honey alongside.

Breakfast 02 · 12 minutes, one pan

Spinach, Feta & Cherry Tomato Omelette with Oregano & Wholemeal Toast

A Greek island taverna omelette: wilted spinach, blistered tomatoes and salty pockets of feta, folded soft and finished with dried oregano. The extra whites push the protein to serious numbers without turning it into a rubber mat.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 5 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 8 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
430kcal
32gprotein
25gcarbs
22gfat
6gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 5 large eggs
  • 2 egg whites (or 60ml carton whites)
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 120g baby spinach
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 60g feta, crumbled
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 2 slices wholemeal seeded bread (~80g)
  • 5g butter for the toast
  • Salt (very little — the feta brings plenty) & black pepper

Equipment

  • 24cm non-stick frying pan
  • Silicone spatula
  • Mixing bowl & whisk
  • Toaster

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs and whites with a good grind of pepper and the smallest pinch of salt until just combined — stop before it goes frothy.
  2. Heat half the oil in the pan over medium-high. Add the tomatoes cut-side down and leave them untouched for 2 minutes until blistered and juicy. Add the spinach and toss for 30 seconds until it collapses. Tip everything onto a plate.
  3. Bread in the toaster now.
  4. Wipe the pan, add the remaining oil over medium heat, and pour in half the egg. Swirl, then use the spatula to draw the setting edges into the middle, tilting the pan so raw egg runs into the gaps — 60–90 seconds until the top is still slightly glossy.
  5. Scatter half the spinach, tomatoes and feta over one side, dust with oregano, fold, and slide onto a warm plate. Repeat for the second omelette (the second one is always better; give it to whoever had a worse morning).
  6. Butter the toast thinly and serve alongside, with more black pepper over everything.

Chef’s tip

Take the pan off the heat while the omelette still looks 10% underdone — carry-over heat finishes it on the plate. An overcooked omelette is just scrambled eggs with regrets.

Make ahead

Blister the tomatoes and wilt the spinach the night before; fridge them, then the morning job is 4 minutes of eggs.

Storage

Cooked omelette doesn’t keep gracefully — eat fresh. The filling keeps 2 days in the fridge.

Freezing

Not suitable — cooked egg turns rubbery from frozen.

For your daughter

Two slices of toast to your one, buttered properly, plus a glass of whole milk or orange juice. Same omelette.

Breakfast 03 · Pre-training fuel · Zero morning effort

Apple & Cinnamon Bircher Oats with Greek Yogurt, Sultanas & Toasted Walnuts

Proper Swiss-style Bircher: oats and grated apple softened overnight in milk and yogurt until pudding-creamy, sharpened with lemon and warmed with cinnamon. Built the night before in three minutes; eaten before Tuesday’s barbell without any thinking at all.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 8 min (the night before)
  • 🔥 Cook: none
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
445kcal
30gprotein
55gcarbs
12gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 90g porridge oats
  • 1 eating apple (~150g), coarsely grated, skin on
  • 200ml semi-skimmed milk
  • 250g 0% fat Greek yogurt (plus 50g to top)
  • 20g sultanas
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • ½ lemon, juice only
  • 10g honey
  • Pinch fine sea salt
  • 20g walnuts, toasted & roughly chopped
  • 80g fresh or frozen berries, to top

Equipment

  • Box grater
  • 2 × 500ml jars or one bowl with a lid
  • Small dry frying pan (for the walnuts)

Method

  1. The night before: grate the apple straight into a bowl, skin and all, and immediately toss with the lemon juice so it stays bright.
  2. Stir in the oats, milk, 250g of the yogurt, sultanas, cinnamon, honey and salt. It should look sloppy — the oats will drink most of it overnight.
  3. Divide between two jars, lid on, fridge.
  4. Toast the walnuts in a dry pan over medium heat for 2–3 minutes until fragrant. Keep them in a separate jar — they go on in the morning, never overnight.
  5. In the morning: stir, loosening with a splash of milk if it has set too thick. It should fall lazily off the spoon.
  6. Top with the extra yogurt, the berries and the walnuts. Eat, lift, conquer.

Chef’s tip

The lemon juice isn’t optional — it stops the apple browning and cuts the sweetness. Grate the apple on the coarse holes; fine-grated apple disappears into paste.

Make ahead

Entirely a make-ahead recipe. Build two days’ jars at once on Sunday and Wednesday nights.

Storage

Fridge, 3 days. Day two is the best day.

Freezing

Not suitable — the yogurt splits and the apple weeps on defrosting.

For your daughter

Her jar: 60g oats to your 45g, whole milk, full-fat yogurt, and a drizzle more honey. If she trains after school, this makes a perfect second appearance at 4pm.

Breakfast 04 · The 10-minute luxury

Soft Scrambled Eggs with Smoked Salmon, Chives & Lemon on Seeded Wholemeal Toast

Low, slow, French-style scrambled eggs — small curds, barely set — draped with smoked salmon that warms to silk on contact. A squeeze of lemon and a snowfall of chives make it taste like a hotel breakfast that costs £16.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 4 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 6 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
425kcal
33gprotein
28gcarbs
20gfat
5gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 5 large eggs
  • 30ml semi-skimmed milk
  • 10g butter
  • 120g smoked salmon
  • 2 thick slices wholemeal seeded bread (~90g)
  • 1 tbsp fresh chives, finely snipped
  • ½ lemon, cut into wedges
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved, to serve
  • Black pepper (no salt in the eggs — the salmon carries it)

Equipment

  • Small non-stick saucepan
  • Silicone spatula
  • Toaster

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs with the milk and a generous grind of pepper.
  2. Melt the butter in the saucepan over low heat — lower than feels right. Pour in the eggs.
  3. Stir slowly and constantly with the spatula, scraping the base and sides. For the first two minutes nothing will happen. Trust the process.
  4. Bread in the toaster when the first curds form.
  5. When the eggs are thick, glossy and still slightly loose — about 5–6 minutes total — pull the pan off the heat. They finish cooking in their own warmth. Stir in half the chives.
  6. Pile onto the toast (no butter needed — the eggs are rich enough), drape the smoked salmon over the top so it softens against the warmth, and finish with the remaining chives, a small squeeze of lemon and more pepper. Tomatoes on the side.

Chef’s tip

The difference between good and great scrambled eggs is 50% lower heat and pulling the pan off 30 seconds earlier than instinct says. There is no recovering an overcooked scramble.

Make ahead

Nothing to prep — this is the fast luxury of the week. Snip the chives the night before if the morning is tight.

Storage

Eat immediately. Unopened smoked salmon keeps to its date; opened, 2 days wrapped tight.

Freezing

Scrambled eggs: never. Smoked salmon freezes well for a month if you buy extra on offer.

For your daughter

Same plate plus a second slice of toast with butter, and swap her a little extra salmon if she’ll take it — teenage iron and omega-3 intakes are usually the two lowest numbers on the chart.

Breakfast 05 · Sunday, done slowly

Weekend Shakshuka — Eggs Baked in Spiced Tomatoes & Peppers with Feta & Warm Pitta

One pan in the middle of the table, six eggs sunk into a rust-red sauce of slow-cooked peppers, cumin and smoked paprika, feta melting at the edges. The kind of breakfast that makes a Sunday feel like a holiday.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 10 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 30 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 generously
440kcal
28gprotein
38gcarbs
20gfat
9gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 15ml olive oil
  • 1 large brown onion (~150g), finely sliced
  • 2 red peppers (~300g), sliced into strips
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely sliced
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • ¼ tsp chilli flakes (more if you both like heat)
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 100ml water
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 5 large eggs
  • 50g feta, crumbled
  • Small handful flat-leaf parsley or coriander, chopped
  • 2 wholemeal pittas (~120g), warmed
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • 26–28cm deep frying pan or sauté pan with a lid
  • Wooden spoon

Method

  1. Warm the oil in the pan over medium heat. Add the onion and peppers with a pinch of salt and cook, stirring now and then, for 10–12 minutes. Do not rush this — the sweetness of the whole dish is built here, and the peppers should be completely soft and starting to catch.
  2. Add the garlic, cumin, paprika and chilli flakes; cook for 1 minute until the kitchen smells like a Tel Aviv café. Stir in the tomato purée and cook 1 minute more.
  3. Tip in the tomatoes, water and honey. Season, then simmer uncovered for 8–10 minutes until thick enough that a spoon dragged through leaves a trail.
  4. Make five hollows in the sauce with the back of the spoon. Crack an egg into each. Season the eggs, scatter over the feta, and put the lid on.
  5. Cook over low heat for 6–8 minutes, until the whites are completely set but the yolks still tremble. Check at 6 — the line between perfect and hard-boiled is about 90 seconds.
  6. Warm the pittas in the toaster while the eggs finish. Shower the pan with herbs and black pepper, bring the whole thing to the table, and hand out spoons. Him: 2 eggs and half the sauce with one pitta. Her: the rest with more pitta.

Chef’s tip

The honey isn’t sweetness for its own sake — it balances the tinned tomatoes’ acidity. If your sauce tastes sharp, that’s the fix, not more salt.

Make ahead

The sauce (steps 1–3) keeps 3 days in the fridge and improves overnight. Sunday morning becomes: reheat sauce, add eggs, 8 minutes.

Storage

Sauce without eggs: fridge, 3 days. Once the eggs go in, eat it — baked eggs don’t keep.

Freezing

Sauce freezes beautifully for 3 months — make double and bank a lazy future Sunday. Never freeze with the eggs in.

For your daughter

She gets 3 eggs to your 2 if she wants them, both pittas’ worth of dipping plus an extra one warmed, and a glass of orange juice with it (vitamin C roughly doubles the iron she absorbs from the eggs and sauce).

Breakfast 06 · Saturday tradition in the making

Banana & Cottage Cheese Protein Pancakes with Blueberries, Greek Yogurt & Honey

Blender pancakes with a secret: cottage cheese vanishes completely in the batter and leaves behind only protein and an impossibly tender crumb. Stack them high, let the blueberries burst in the pan, and nobody at the table will use the word “healthy”.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 8 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 15 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (about 10 pancakes)
435kcal
32gprotein
48gcarbs
12gfat
6gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

Batter

  • 100g porridge oats
  • 200g cottage cheese
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 ripe banana (~120g peeled)
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • Pinch fine sea salt
  • 10ml light olive oil or butter, for the pan

To serve

  • 150g blueberries
  • 150g 0% fat Greek yogurt
  • 20g honey

Equipment

  • Blender or stick blender
  • Large non-stick frying pan
  • Fish slice / thin spatula
  • Low oven (90°C) to keep the stack warm

Method

  1. Blitz the oats alone in the blender to a rough flour, then add the cottage cheese, eggs, banana, baking powder, vanilla, cinnamon and salt. Blend until completely smooth. Rest the batter for 5 minutes — it thickens as the oats drink.
  2. Heat a whisper of oil in the pan over medium heat. Wipe most of it out with kitchen paper; a nearly-dry pan gives the even, golden face.
  3. Drop in heaped tablespoons of batter to make 8–9cm pancakes, three at a time. Press 3–4 blueberries into the wet top of each.
  4. Cook 2–3 minutes until bubbles appear at the edges and the underside is deep gold. Flip with confidence — hesitant flips fold. Another 90 seconds on the second side.
  5. Keep the finished pancakes in the low oven while you work through the batter.
  6. Stack, top with yogurt and the remaining blueberries, and ribbon the honey over at the table so everyone sees it happen. Him: 4 pancakes. Her: the rest.

Chef’s tip

Medium heat, not high. Protein-rich batters brown faster than flour ones — if the first pancake goes mahogany in 60 seconds, drop the heat and call it the cook’s tax.

Make ahead

The batter keeps overnight in the fridge (it thickens — loosen with a splash of milk). Or cook the full batch and reheat.

Storage

Cooked pancakes: fridge, 3 days, stacked with baking paper between. 20 seconds each in the toaster brings them back better than the microwave.

Freezing

Excellent — freeze flat on a tray, then bag, up to 2 months. Toast from frozen. A freezer bag of these is her best pre-training snack in the book.

For your daughter

Six pancakes to your four, honey un-rationed, and add a glass of whole milk. Double the batch most Saturdays and freeze her a stash.

Breakfast 07 · Out-the-door food

Mediterranean Breakfast Wrap with Soft Scrambled Egg, Charred Pepper, Avocado & Chilli

A warm wholemeal tortilla rolled around soft eggs, sweet charred peppers, cool avocado and a flicker of chilli — structurally sound enough to eat with one hand on a school-run Wednesday.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 6 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 8 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
450kcal
30gprotein
40gcarbs
19gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 5 large eggs
  • 1 red pepper (~150g), sliced into thin strips
  • 1 spring onion, finely sliced (or ¼ red onion)
  • 10ml olive oil
  • ½ ripe avocado (~70g), sliced
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 2 large wholemeal tortilla wraps (~130g)
  • 60g baby spinach
  • ¼ tsp chilli flakes
  • ½ lemon, juice only
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Non-stick frying pan
  • Silicone spatula
  • Foil, if wrapping to go

Method

  1. Get the pan properly hot with half the oil. Throw in the pepper strips and leave them alone for 2 minutes to char at the edges, then toss with the spring onion for 30 seconds and tip out.
  2. Drop the heat to medium-low, add the rest of the oil, and scramble the eggs slowly with a pinch of salt — stop while they still shine.
  3. Warm the wraps: 20 seconds a side in the dry pan (or 15 seconds in the microwave under a damp cloth). A cold wrap cracks; a warm one rolls.
  4. Build down the centre line of each wrap: spinach first (it’s the moisture barrier), then eggs, peppers, avocado and feta. Chilli flakes, lemon juice, black pepper.
  5. Fold the bottom up, then the sides in tight, and roll away from yourself. Halve on the diagonal if eating at the table.
  6. Eating in five minutes’ time? Wrap in foil seam-down — it holds together and stays warm to the school gate.

Chef’s tip

The spinach-first rule matters: leaves between wrap and wet filling keep the tortilla from going soggy. And char the peppers hard — that smoky edge is the flavour of the whole wrap.

Make ahead

Char the peppers the night before. Eggs and assembly are a 5-minute morning job.

Storage

Assembled wraps go soft within hours — store components separately (peppers 3 days, fridge) and build fresh.

Freezing

Not suitable assembled (avocado and egg both object). Wraps themselves freeze for 3 months.

For your daughter

The other half of the avocado goes in hers, plus a banana in the bag. If it’s a match day, make hers a double-wrap — two smaller wraps roll easier than one overstuffed one.
