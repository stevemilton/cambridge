Lunch 01 · Training-day fuel · Cook once, eat twice

Chicken Souvlaki Bowl with Lemon-Herb Rice, Cucumber Salad & Proper Tzatziki

The Athens street classic, rebuilt as a bowl: oregano-and-lemon marinated chicken charred hard in a screaming pan, over rice that’s been taught to taste of lemon, with cold tzatziki and a shattering cucumber salad. Make double — Tuesday-you says thanks.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min (+30 min–24h marinating)
  • 🔥 Cook: 25 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (double everything for 4 portions)
545kcal
44gprotein
52gcarbs
16gfat
6gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

Chicken & marinade

  • 350g chicken breast, cut into 3cm chunks
  • 15ml olive oil
  • 1 lemon — zest & half the juice
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • Salt & black pepper

Lemon-herb rice

  • 130g white basmati rice (dry)
  • 1 low-salt chicken stock cube
  • Remaining lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley & dill

Tzatziki (makes extra — it keeps)

  • 200g 0% fat Greek yogurt
  • ¼ cucumber (~100g), coarsely grated
  • 1 small garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 5ml extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tbsp chopped mint or dill

Cucumber salad

  • ½ cucumber, halved & sliced
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ red onion, paper-thin
  • 6 Kalamata olives, halved
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar

Equipment

  • Griddle pan or heavy frying pan
  • Medium saucepan with lid
  • Box grater
  • Sieve (for the cucumber)

Method

  1. Marinate. Toss the chicken with the oil, lemon zest, half the lemon juice, garlic, oregano, paprika, ½ tsp salt and plenty of pepper. Thirty minutes on the counter works; overnight in the fridge is better.
  2. Tzatziki. Grate the cucumber, toss with a pinch of salt, and leave it in a sieve for 10 minutes. Then squeeze it hard in your fist — this is the difference between tzatziki and yogurt soup. Stir into the yogurt with the garlic, vinegar, oil and herbs. Fridge until needed.
  3. Rice. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear. Cook with the stock cube in plenty of water for 10–12 minutes until just tender; drain, then fork through the lemon juice and herbs with a grind of pepper.
  4. Char the chicken. Get the griddle pan smoking-hot — properly hot, windows-open hot. Shake excess marinade off the chicken and lay the pieces down in one layer. Leave 3 minutes untouched for deep char lines, turn, and cook 3–4 minutes more until cooked through (no pink, juices clear, 74°C+ if you use a thermometer).
  5. Salad. Toss the cucumber, tomatoes, onion and olives with the vinegar and a pinch of salt.
  6. Build. Rice in the base, chicken over one side, salad on the other, a thick spoonful of tzatziki in the middle. Black pepper and a last few drops of lemon over the chicken.

Chef’s tip

Dry, hot and undisturbed: the three rules of char. Wet chicken in a timid pan steams grey. If the marinade drips, pat the pieces with kitchen paper first.

Make ahead

Marinade + tzatziki on Sunday. The whole bowl also builds brilliantly as a boxed lunch — keep the tzatziki in a corner pot until eating.

Storage

Cooked chicken 3 days; rice 24 hours only (cooled fast — see Chapter 06); tzatziki 3–4 days; salad best same day.

Freezing

Raw marinated chicken freezes for 3 months (freeze it in the marinade). Cooked components: don’t bother — the fresh version is 25 minutes away.

For your daughter

Her bowl gets 200–225g cooked rice to your 150g, plus a warm pitta on the side. Same chicken, same tzatziki, extra olives if she’s that kind of teenager.

Lunch 02 · No cooking · 15 minutes flat

Tuscan Tuna & Cannellini Bean Salad with Sun-Dried Tomatoes, Red Onion & Lemon

The great Italian storecupboard lunch: creamy white beans and good tuna under a sharp lemon-and-olive-oil dressing, with sun-dried tomatoes doing the heavy lifting. It costs about £2.50 a portion and eats like antipasti.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: none
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (double it — it improves overnight)
490kcal
40gprotein
38gcarbs
18gfat
11gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 2 × 145g tins tuna in spring water, drained (~200g drained)
  • 1 × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained & rinsed
  • 40g sun-dried tomatoes in oil, drained & sliced
  • ½ red onion, very finely sliced
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, quartered
  • ½ cucumber, diced
  • 60g rocket or mixed leaves
  • 10 Kalamata olives, halved
  • 2 tbsp flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • 2 slices wholemeal seeded bread (~80g), to serve

Dressing

  • 20ml extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 lemon, juice + ½ its zest
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 small garlic clove, crushed
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Large mixing bowl
  • Small jar with a lid (dressing)
  • Sieve

Method

  1. Put the sliced red onion in a small bowl with a squeeze of the lemon and a pinch of salt. Leave it while you do everything else — five minutes of quick-pickling turns raw onion from aggressive to essential.
  2. Shake all the dressing ingredients together in the jar until emulsified and creamy-looking.
  3. Tumble the beans, sun-dried tomatoes, cherry tomatoes, cucumber and olives into the big bowl. Pour over two-thirds of the dressing and fold gently — the beans should stay whole.
  4. Flake in the tuna in fat, generous pieces. Fold once, no more; over-mixed tuna turns to paste.
  5. Add the pickled onion, parsley and leaves, and the last of the dressing. One final gentle turn.
  6. Serve heaped, with bread for the dressing at the bottom of the bowl — the best bite of the whole lunch.

Chef’s tip

Season the beans directly and taste before the tuna goes in — beans are flavour sponges and under-seasoned beans read as “diet food”. This salad should taste emphatic.

Make ahead

Everything except the leaves and parsley can be dressed and boxed the night before — it genuinely improves. Add leaves when serving.

Storage

Fridge, 2 days (undressed leaves separate). Give it 10 minutes out of the fridge before eating — cold mutes the dressing.

Freezing

Not suitable — nothing here wants to be frozen, and nothing needs to be.

For your daughter

Hers comes with double bread (4 slices, or a buttered ciabatta roll) and an extra drizzle of olive oil through the beans. If she’s lukewarm on tuna, mozzarella pearls work in her half.

Lunch 03 · Training-day fuel

Harissa Chicken with Jewelled Roast Vegetable Couscous, Almonds & Mint Yogurt

Sticky harissa-glazed chicken over couscous studded with sweet roasted peppers, courgette and red onion, apricots and toasted almonds — finished with cooling mint yogurt. North African heat, ready in half an hour.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 25 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
540kcal
42gprotein
55gcarbs
14gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

Chicken

  • 320g chicken breast, butterflied
  • 1 tbsp harissa paste
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 10ml olive oil
  • ½ lemon, juice only

Couscous & vegetables

  • 100g wholewheat couscous (dry)
  • 150ml hot chicken stock
  • 1 red pepper, chunks
  • 1 courgette (~200g), half-moons
  • 1 red onion, wedges
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 4 dried apricots (~30g), sliced
  • 15g flaked almonds, toasted
  • 2 tbsp each mint & parsley, chopped

Mint yogurt

  • 100g 0% fat Greek yogurt
  • 1 tbsp mint, chopped
  • Squeeze of lemon, pinch of salt

Equipment

  • Large baking tray
  • Griddle or frying pan
  • Heatproof bowl with a plate to cover

Method

  1. Oven to 210°C fan. Toss the pepper, courgette and onion with the oil, cumin and a pinch of salt on the tray. Roast 20–22 minutes until the edges blacken sweetly.
  2. Mix the harissa, honey, oil and lemon; massage into the butterflied chicken. Leave while the vegetables roast.
  3. Couscous into the bowl, hot stock over, plate on top, 8 minutes. Then fork it light — never stir couscous, fluff it.
  4. Griddle the chicken over high heat, 3–4 minutes per side until charred at the edges and cooked through. Rest 3 minutes under foil, then slice thickly on the angle.
  5. Fold the roasted vegetables, apricots, almonds and herbs through the couscous. Taste — it usually wants more lemon and a grind of pepper.
  6. Couscous down first, chicken over, mint yogurt spooned across, and any resting juices poured on top like you mean it.

Chef’s tip

Harissa brands vary wildly in heat. Taste yours: mild rose harissa can go in at 1½ tbsp; a fierce tube might want 2 tsp. The honey rounds whichever you use.

Make ahead

Roast the vegetables and marinate the chicken up to 2 days ahead. Assembled boxes (chicken cooked) keep for next-day lunches.

Storage

Everything keeps 3 days in the fridge, together or apart. Eat cold or 90 seconds in the microwave — both work.

Freezing

Raw harissa chicken freezes in its marinade for 3 months. Couscous and roast veg: fridge food, not freezer food.

For your daughter

Make 150g couscous instead of 100g and give her the difference (roughly 90g cooked extra), plus a warmed flatbread. Go gentle-half on the harissa if she’s heat-shy — glaze her piece with the honey-lemon mix and a pinch of paprika instead.

Lunch 04 · Saturday street food

Greek Chicken Pittas with Quick Tzatziki, Pickled Slaw & Charred Lemon

Gyros energy without the rotating spit: oregano chicken thigh-style strips, warm pittas, garlicky tzatziki and a sharp cabbage slaw that cuts through everything. Assembly happens at the table, which is half the fun.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min (+30 min marinating)
  • 🔥 Cook: 12 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
520kcal
42gprotein
48gcarbs
15gfat
7gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 320g chicken breast, sliced into finger-width strips
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp sweet smoked paprika
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 lemon — half juiced, half in wedges for charring
  • 2 large wholemeal pittas (~120g)
  • 150g tzatziki (see Lunch 01 — or the weekly batch)
  • 2 large tomatoes, sliced
  • Salt & black pepper

Pickled slaw

  • 150g red cabbage, shredded fine
  • 1 carrot (~80g), coarsely grated
  • ¼ red onion, fine slices
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • ½ tsp honey, pinch of salt

Equipment

  • Heavy frying pan or griddle
  • Mixing bowls
  • Tongs

Method

  1. Toss the chicken strips with the oil, oregano, paprika, garlic, lemon juice, ½ tsp salt and pepper. Thirty minutes if you have it; even ten helps.
  2. Scrunch the slaw ingredients together with your hands in a bowl — actually scrunch, for a minute, until the cabbage relaxes and turns glossy. Set aside; it gets better as it sits.
  3. Heat the dry pan to very hot. Chicken strips in a single layer — 2–3 minutes per side until burnished and cooked through. Work in two batches rather than crowding.
  4. In the same pan, press the lemon wedges cut-side down for 60 seconds until caramelised, and warm the pittas for 30 seconds a side in the chicken’s residue. Waste nothing.
  5. To the table: pittas halved into pockets, chicken, slaw, tomato slices, tzatziki, charred lemon for squeezing.
  6. Build order for structural integrity: tzatziki inside the pocket walls first, then chicken, then slaw and tomato. Squeeze of burnt lemon. Napkins.

Chef’s tip

Charring the lemon is 60 seconds of effort for a completely different squeeze — sweeter, smokier, more of it. Once you start you won’t stop.

Make ahead

Slaw and tzatziki both hold 2 days and both improve. Chicken can marinate overnight.

Storage

Cooked chicken, 3 days. Slaw, 3 days (it softens into more of a pickle — still excellent). Assembled pittas: eat now.

Freezing

Raw marinated chicken, 3 months. Pittas freeze; slaw and tzatziki don’t.

For your daughter

Two pittas for her, one for you. Same everything else — this is already teenager-perfect food.

Lunch 05 · Sunday lunch, French rules

Warm Salmon Niçoise with New Potatoes, Green Beans, Soft-Boiled Eggs & Dijon Dressing

The Riviera salad done properly: warm crushed new potatoes, squeaky green beans, jammy-yolked eggs and a fillet of roast salmon over crisp leaves, all pulled together by a mustardy dressing with real attitude.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 20 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
550kcal
38gprotein
35gcarbs
26gfat
7gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 2 × 130g salmon fillets, skin on
  • 300g baby new potatoes, halved
  • 200g green beans, trimmed
  • 3 large eggs
  • 2 Little Gem lettuces, quartered lengthways
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 10 Kalamata olives
  • 1 tsp capers, rinsed
  • 5ml olive oil (for the salmon)
  • Salt & black pepper

Dijon dressing

  • 20ml extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 1 small shallot or ¼ red onion, minced
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Medium saucepan
  • Baking tray
  • Bowl of iced water
  • Jar for the dressing

Method

  1. Oven to 200°C fan. Potatoes into cold salted water, bring to the boil, 12–14 minutes until a knife slides in.
  2. Meanwhile: salmon on the tray, skin-side down, rubbed with the oil, salt and pepper. Roast 12–14 minutes until it just flakes — it should still look slightly translucent at the very centre; it finishes as it rests.
  3. Eggs into the potato pan for the final 7 minutes exactly, then straight into iced water. Beans into the same pan for the last 3 minutes. (One pan, three jobs.) Drain everything.
  4. Shake the dressing hard in its jar until thick. Peel and halve the eggs — yolks should be fudgy, not chalky.
  5. While the potatoes are warm, crush each one slightly with a fork and toss with a third of the dressing. Warm potatoes drink dressing; cold ones just wear it.
  6. Arrange: leaves and beans, dressed potatoes, tomatoes, olives and capers, salmon broken into large flakes (skin discarded or crisped for the cook), eggs on top. Remaining dressing over everything, pepper, done.

Chef’s tip

Seven minutes for the eggs — set an actual timer. The gap between “jammy centrepiece” and “sad picnic egg” is under a minute.

Make ahead

Eggs, beans, potatoes and dressing all hold 2 days, making the boxed-lunch version a 5-minute build. Cook the salmon fresh, or use it deliberately cold.

Storage

Assembled (undressed): 1 day. Cooked salmon: 2 days, eaten cold from the fridge — cold roast salmon is genuinely great here.

Freezing

Not suitable as a dish. Buy salmon frozen for economy and defrost overnight before cooking.

For your daughter

Add 150g more potatoes to the pan for her portion and a buttered slice of baguette or wholemeal on the side. If capers and olives are a bridge too far, they lift off her half without harm.

Lunch 06 · Batch hero · Freezer favourite

Turkey & Courgette Koftas with Bulgur Tabbouleh & Whipped Minted Yogurt

Grated courgette keeps lean turkey mince impossibly juicy; cumin, mint and lemon zest make it taste of the Eastern Mediterranean. Served over a proper tabbouleh — more herbs than grain, the way it’s meant to be.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 20 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 15 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (recipe makes 12 koftas — freeze half)
530kcal
41gprotein
45gcarbs
17gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (12 koftas / 4 portions)

Koftas

  • 500g turkey mince (2% fat)
  • 1 courgette (~200g), coarsely grated & squeezed dry
  • 1 small onion, grated
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • ½ tsp cinnamon
  • 3 tbsp mint & parsley, chopped
  • 1 lemon, zest only
  • 1 egg
  • 30g porridge oats, blitzed fine
  • 1 tsp salt, plenty of pepper
  • 10ml olive oil, for frying

Tabbouleh (serves 2)

  • 90g bulgur wheat (dry)
  • 200g cherry tomatoes, chopped small
  • ½ cucumber, diced fine
  • 40g flat-leaf parsley (a whole bunch), chopped
  • 15g mint leaves, chopped
  • 3 spring onions, sliced
  • 15ml extra virgin olive oil + 1 lemon, juiced

Whipped minted yogurt

  • 150g 0% fat Greek yogurt, whisked with 1 tbsp chopped mint, a squeeze of lemon & a pinch of salt

Equipment

  • Box grater & clean tea towel
  • Large bowl
  • Large frying pan
  • Heatproof bowl for the bulgur

Method

  1. Bulgur into a bowl, cover with 180ml boiling water and a pinch of salt, clingfilm over, 15 minutes. Then fluff and cool.
  2. The kofta make-or-break step: grate the courgette, pile it into a tea towel, and wring it out over the sink like you’re angry at it. You’ll get several tablespoons of water out — water that would otherwise steam your koftas apart.
  3. Mix the turkey, courgette, onion, garlic, spices, herbs, lemon zest, egg, oats, salt and pepper. Knead for a full minute — this develops the bind. Fry a teaspoon of the mix, taste, and fix the seasoning now, not later.
  4. Shape into 12 torpedo-shaped koftas with wet hands. Fridge for 10 minutes if you have them — cold koftas hold better.
  5. Heat the oil over medium-high. Fry the koftas 10–12 minutes, turning to brown all sides, until cooked through (no pink, 74°C+). Don’t crowd; two batches beats one grey one.
  6. Fold the tomatoes, cucumber, herbs, spring onion, oil and lemon through the bulgur; season assertively. Plate: tabbouleh, three koftas each, whipped yogurt, pepper, a last squeeze of lemon.

Chef’s tip

Always fry the tester teaspoon (step 3). Every kofta failure — bland, loose, over-salted — is caught at that moment for the price of one bite.

Make ahead

Kofta mix keeps raw for 24h shaped in the fridge. Tabbouleh grain-and-tomato base holds a day; fold herbs in fresh.

Storage

Cooked koftas: 3 days fridge. Tabbouleh: 2 days. Yogurt: 3 days. All three travel well as a lunchbox.

Freezing

The point of the recipe: cooked koftas freeze superbly for 3 months. Open-freeze on a tray, then bag. Reheat covered at 180°C for 10–12 minutes.

For your daughter

Four koftas to your three, 50% more bulgur (make 130g dry and split it 60/40 her way), and a warm pitta. This is also her favourite packed lunch — send the yogurt in a little pot.

Lunch 07 · Training-day fuel · 20 minutes

Zesty Prawn & Avocado Brown Rice Bowl with Chilli, Lime, Cucumber & Toasted Seeds

Somewhere between a poke bowl and the best prawn cocktail you’ve ever had: sweet garlicky prawns still warm from the pan over nutty brown rice, with cold avocado, cucumber and a dressing that bites back.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 12 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 8 min (plus rice)
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
520kcal
36gprotein
55gcarbs
16gfat
9gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 300g raw king prawns, deveined
  • 140g brown basmati rice (dry) — or 280g from the Sunday batch
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • ½ fresh red chilli, fine slices
  • 1 ripe avocado (~140g flesh), sliced
  • ½ cucumber, ribboned with a peeler
  • 1 carrot, ribboned
  • 100g frozen edamame or peas, blanched 2 min
  • 15g mixed seeds, toasted
  • Handful coriander leaves

Dressing

  • 2 limes, juice (or 1 lemon)
  • 10ml reduced-salt soy sauce
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 5ml extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp finely grated ginger

Equipment

  • Frying pan or wok
  • Vegetable peeler
  • Small jar for dressing

Method

  1. Rice on first if cooking fresh: rinse, then simmer in plenty of salted water for 22–25 minutes; drain well. (Sunday-batch rice: reheat until piping hot.)
  2. Shake the dressing together. Taste — it should be sharp enough to make you blink, because the rice and avocado will soften it.
  3. Pat the prawns bone-dry. Wet prawns boil; dry prawns caramelise.
  4. Oil into a very hot pan; prawns in one layer, 90 seconds untouched until pink and blistered underneath. Add the garlic and chilli, toss for 60–90 seconds more until the prawns just curl into loose Cs. A tight C is overcooked — stop at loose.
  5. Divide the warm rice between bowls and spoon a third of the dressing straight into it.
  6. Arrange the avocado, cucumber, carrot and edamame in sections. Prawns (and every drop of their pan juices) in the middle. Remaining dressing over, then seeds and coriander.

Chef’s tip

Buy frozen raw king prawns — half the price of fresh, identical in the pan. Defrost in a bowl of cold water in 15 minutes, then dry thoroughly.

Make ahead

Rice, dressing, ribbons and blanched edamame all hold overnight. Prawns take 3 minutes — always cook them at the last moment.

Storage

Cooked prawns: 2 days, eaten cold (they’re good cold in this bowl). Rice: 24-hour rule, always. Cut avocado: same day.

Freezing

Not suitable assembled. Raw prawns live in the freezer anyway — that’s the system working.

For your daughter

Her bowl: 100g more cooked rice, the bigger half of the avocado, and go easy-or-onside with the chilli. A handful of prawn crackers on top makes her actively request this lunch.

Lunch 08 · Batch hero · Freezer favourite

Hearty Chicken Minestrone with Wholemeal Pasta, White Beans & a Parmesan-Basil Finish

A minestrone with a job to do: proper soffritto base, shredded chicken, cannellini beans and wholemeal pasta in a tomato broth that tastes like it simmered all afternoon (it took forty minutes). One pot makes four generous portions — half goes to the freezer.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 40 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 4 (eat 2, freeze 2)
495kcal
40gprotein
48gcarbs
13gfat
10gfibre

Ingredients (serves 4)

  • 15ml olive oil
  • 1 large onion, diced fine
  • 2 carrots (~180g), diced
  • 2 celery sticks, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano + 1 bay leaf
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1.4 litres chicken stock (2 low-salt cubes)
  • 500g chicken breast, whole
  • 1 × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 120g wholemeal pasta (small shapes, or spaghetti snapped short)
  • 1 courgette, diced
  • 100g green beans, cut into thirds
  • 80g baby spinach
  • 20g Parmesan, finely grated
  • Handful basil leaves, torn
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Large soup pot or casserole (4L+)
  • Two forks (for shredding)
  • Ladle

Method

  1. Soffritto, unhurried. Oil into the pot over medium heat; onion, carrot and celery with a pinch of salt for 8–10 minutes until soft, sweet and golden at the edges. This is the flavour foundation of the whole pot — the one step you cannot rush.
  2. Garlic, oregano and bay for 1 minute; tomato purée for 1 more, stirring, until it darkens a shade.
  3. Tomatoes and stock in. Lower the whole chicken breasts into the broth, bring to a gentle simmer, and poach — lid half-on — for 15 minutes.
  4. Lift the chicken onto a board and shred it with two forks into rough, generous strands.
  5. Pasta, beans, courgette and green beans into the pot; simmer 9–10 minutes until the pasta is just short of done. Return the chicken for the final 2 minutes with the spinach, which collapses on contact.
  6. Taste and season boldly — soups eat more salt and pepper than you expect. Ladle into wide bowls; Parmesan and torn basil over each. Freeze the rest (see notes).

Chef’s tip

Poaching the breasts whole in the broth, then shredding, gives silkier chicken than dicing and boiling ever will — and the poaching seasons the broth as it goes.

Make ahead

The whole point. Day two is deeper. If making deliberately ahead, hold the spinach and add on reheat.

Storage

Fridge, 3 days. The pasta drinks broth as it sits — loosen with hot water or stock when reheating, and re-season.

Freezing

3 months in portion tubs. Best practice: freeze slightly before the pasta is fully cooked and finish it on reheat. Reheat to a full rolling simmer.

For your daughter

Bigger ladle, extra Parmesan, and thick-cut buttered wholemeal toast or garlic bread for dunking. Soup with nothing to dunk is, in her words, a beverage.

Lunch 09 · Weekend brunch energy · Vegetarian

Spanish Pepper & Potato Tortilla with Smoked Paprika, Rocket & Toasted Almond Salad

The great Spanish omelette — layers of tender potato and sweet pepper set in golden egg, served in fat wedges at room temperature like they do in Seville, with a peppery rocket salad and crunchy almonds. Magnificent hot, arguably better three hours later.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 35 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 for lunch + 2 boxed slices
510kcal
30gprotein
42gcarbs
24gfat
6gfibre

Ingredients (serves 4)

  • 8 large eggs
  • 550g white potatoes, peeled, quartered & sliced 3mm thin
  • 2 red peppers, sliced
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 25ml olive oil
  • 1 tsp sweet smoked paprika
  • Salt & black pepper

Salad (per 2 servings)

  • 80g rocket
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 20g whole almonds, toasted & roughly chopped
  • 10ml extra virgin olive oil + 1 tsp balsamic vinegar

Equipment

  • 24cm non-stick frying pan (the right size matters — a 28cm pan makes a pancake)
  • A large flat plate or board, for The Flip
  • Slotted spoon

Method

  1. Heat 20ml of the oil in the pan over medium-low. Add the potatoes, onion and a good pinch of salt. Cook gently — more poach than fry — for 18–20 minutes, turning occasionally, until the potatoes are completely tender but barely coloured. Add the peppers for the final 8 minutes.
  2. Whisk the eggs in a large bowl with the paprika, ¾ tsp salt and pepper.
  3. Tip the hot potato mixture into the eggs (not the other way round) and rest for 5 minutes — the potatoes drink egg and the tortilla becomes custardy rather than layered.
  4. Wipe the pan, add the last 5ml oil over medium heat, and pour everything back in. Cook 6–8 minutes, running a spatula round the edge, until the sides are set and the middle still wobbles.
  5. The Flip: plate over the pan, one confident inversion (both hands, over the sink, commit), then slide the tortilla back in raw-side down for 3–4 minutes. The centre should stay ever so slightly juicy — a Spaniard would insist.
  6. Rest 10 minutes minimum. Toss the salad with the oil, vinegar and almonds. Cut fat wedges. Room temperature is the correct temperature.

Chef’s tip

Frightened of The Flip? Slide the pan under a hot grill for 3 minutes instead — technically a tortilla crime, practically identical.

Make ahead

Built for it. Make in the morning for lunch, or the night before. Never serve fridge-cold — 30 minutes on the counter first.

Storage

Fridge, 3 days, wrapped. A wedge in her lunchbox with cherry tomatoes is one of the best packed lunches in the book.

Freezing

Not recommended — frozen egg-and-potato turns watery and grainy on defrosting.

For your daughter

A bigger wedge (cut it 60/40), crusty bread with olive oil, and hers is arguably improved by a little grated Manchego or cheddar over the hot surface.

Lunch 10 · Training-day fuel

Moroccan-Spiced Chicken & Chickpea Grain Bowl with Roasted Carrots, Sultanas & Lemon Tahini-Style Yogurt

Cumin-crusted chicken and burnished roast carrots over bulgur with crispy spiced chickpeas, sweet sultanas and a lemony yogurt drizzle. A bowl with about six textures, which is the entire secret of bowls.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 30 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
540kcal
41gprotein
56gcarbs
14gfat
11gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 320g chicken breast, butterflied
  • 2 tsp ras el hanout
  • 15ml olive oil, divided
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained & patted very dry
  • ½ tsp ground cumin + ½ tsp smoked paprika (for the chickpeas)
  • 3 carrots (~250g), halved lengthways
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 80g bulgur wheat (dry)
  • 20g sultanas
  • 60g baby spinach
  • 2 tbsp coriander & mint, chopped
  • Salt & black pepper

Lemon yogurt drizzle

  • 120g 0% fat Greek yogurt
  • ½ lemon, juice + zest
  • 1 small garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 tsp extra virgin olive oil

Equipment

  • Large baking tray
  • Griddle or frying pan
  • Heatproof bowl for the bulgur

Method

  1. Oven to 200°C fan. Carrots on one half of the tray with 5ml oil, the honey and salt; dried chickpeas on the other half with 5ml oil, cumin, paprika and salt. Roast 25–28 minutes, shaking the chickpea side twice, until the carrots are caramelised and the chickpeas crackle.
  2. Bulgur into the bowl with the sultanas (they plump as it soaks), 160ml boiling water and a pinch of salt. Cover 15 minutes, then fluff.
  3. Rub the chicken with the ras el hanout, remaining 5ml oil and ½ tsp salt. Griddle over high heat 3–4 minutes per side until deeply crusted and cooked through. Rest under foil, then slice.
  4. Whisk the yogurt drizzle ingredients with a splash of cold water until it pours like single cream.
  5. Fold the spinach and herbs through the warm bulgur — the leaves half-wilt, which is exactly right.
  6. Build: bulgur, carrots, sliced chicken, a landslide of crispy chickpeas, yogurt zig-zagged over the whole thing. Resting juices on top.

Chef’s tip

The chickpeas only crisp if they start bone-dry — roll them in a tea towel first and discard any loose skins. Damp chickpeas just bake into sad bullets.

Make ahead

Everything but the chicken holds 2 days (chickpeas lose crunch overnight but re-crisp in 5 oven minutes). Great boxed lunch: yogurt in a corner pot.

Storage

Cooked chicken 3 days; bulgur 2 days; drizzle 3 days.

Freezing

Not suitable assembled. Raw spiced chicken freezes for 3 months.

For your daughter

Make 120g bulgur and give her the extra (about 100g cooked more than you), a warmed flatbread, and let her have the lion’s share of the chickpeas — they’re the bit teenagers fight over.

Lunch 11 · Cook once, eat twice

Loaded Baked Sweet Potatoes with Lemon-Oregano Chicken, Feta, Spinach & Tzatziki

A sweet potato roasted until its edges toffee, split open and piled with warm Greek-spiced chicken, wilted spinach, cold tzatziki and salty feta. Comfort food that happens to hit every macro on the sheet.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 10 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 50 min (mostly oven-idle)
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (roast 4 potatoes, cook double chicken — tomorrow is done)
535kcal
42gprotein
52gcarbs
15gfat
9gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 2 sweet potatoes (~300g each)
  • 320g chicken breast, 2cm dice
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 1½ tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp sweet smoked paprika
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • ½ lemon, juice + zest
  • 120g baby spinach
  • 60g feta, crumbled
  • 120g tzatziki (the weekly batch — see Lunch 01)
  • 1 tbsp chopped dill or parsley
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Baking tray
  • Frying pan
  • Fork (for the all-important scruffing)

Method

  1. Oven to 200°C fan. Prick the sweet potatoes, rub the skins with a few drops of oil and flaky salt, and bake directly on the oven shelf (tray below to catch drips) for 45–55 minutes, until a knife meets zero resistance and caramel leaks from the prick-holes. That leak is the sign of a properly baked sweet potato.
  2. Fifteen minutes before the potatoes are done: toss the chicken with the oil, oregano, paprika, garlic, lemon zest, salt and pepper.
  3. Sear the chicken in a hot pan, 6–8 minutes, tossing until golden on the outside and cooked through. Squeeze the lemon juice into the pan at the end and let it bubble into a 30-second glaze.
  4. Throw the spinach into the same pan; it wilts in the residual heat in under a minute.
  5. Split the potatoes lengthways without cutting through, and scruff the flesh with a fork — ridges catch toppings; smooth flesh sheds them. Season the flesh itself.
  6. Load: chicken and spinach, feta over the warm top so it half-melts, tzatziki last, herbs and a final grind of pepper.

Chef’s tip

Never microwave-“bake” the potato for this one — the oven’s slow roast is what converts the starches to caramel. If time is short, oven-bake on Sunday and re-crisp for 10 minutes on the day.

Make ahead

Baked potatoes and cooked chicken both hold 3 days — this is the plan’s Monday-and-Wednesday lunch for a reason. Reheat both, top cold things fresh.

Storage

Components separately, fridge: potatoes 3 days, chicken 3 days, tzatziki 3–4 days.

Freezing

Baked sweet potato flesh freezes fine (1 month) but the dish is better run on the fridge cycle.

For your daughter

She gets the bigger potato (pick a ~400g one for her at the shop) plus extra feta, and a buttered roll if it’s a training day. Sweet potato + feta is usually a teenage-approval slam dunk.

Lunch 12 · 15 minutes · Sunday-paper food

Smoked Salmon, Soft-Boiled Egg & Smashed Avocado Open Sandwiches with Pickled Onion & Dill

Scandinavian café lunch: rye-style seeded bread under lemony smashed avocado, ribbons of smoked salmon, a jammy egg and quick-pickled red onion. It looks like it was made for a magazine and takes a quarter of an hour.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 12 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 7 min (the eggs)
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
505kcal
34gprotein
36gcarbs
24gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 4 slices wholemeal seeded bread or rye (~160g)
  • 160g smoked salmon
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 ripe avocado (~140g flesh)
  • ½ lemon, juice + zest
  • ¼ red onion, paper-thin slices
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar + ½ tsp honey (for pickling)
  • 1 tbsp fresh dill, fronds picked
  • 1 tsp capers, rinsed (optional, correct)
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • 60g cucumber, shaved into ribbons
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Small saucepan
  • Toaster
  • Fork & bowl (avocado)
  • Small bowl (pickle)

Method

  1. First move: onion into the vinegar, honey and a pinch of salt. By assembly time it will be pink, crisp and completely tamed.
  2. Eggs into boiling water for exactly 7 minutes, then iced water. Peel and halve — the yolks should be molten at the centre, set at the edge.
  3. Smash the avocado roughly with the lemon juice and zest, a pinch of salt and the chilli flakes. Rough means rough — it should have geography, not be a purée.
  4. Toast the bread properly dark. Structural integrity and flavour both live in a serious toasting.
  5. Build each slice: thick layer of avocado to the very edges, folds (not flat sheets) of smoked salmon, cucumber ribbons tucked between.
  6. Crown with egg halves, pickled onion, capers, dill and black pepper. Eat with a knife and fork and unearned smugness.

Chef’s tip

Fold the salmon into waves rather than laying it flat — it eats more luxuriously and looks twice as generous. Every open-sandwich café in Copenhagen knows this.

Make ahead

Pickled onion keeps a week in the fridge and improves. Eggs can be boiled up to 3 days ahead (unpeeled). Avocado: on the day, always.

Storage

Assembled: eat immediately, this is à-la-minute food. Components: per their own rules.

Freezing

Not applicable — nothing here should meet a freezer except spare bread.

For your daughter

Three slices to your two — or two plus a bowl of soup in winter. If smoked salmon isn’t her thing yet, hers works with extra egg and a little grated cheddar under the avocado.
