Dinner 01 · The signature

Garlic & Lemon Chicken with Crispy Herb Potatoes & Charred Tenderstem Broccoli

The dish this whole book was built to prove: chicken breast, potatoes and broccoli can be the best thing you eat all week. Butterflied chicken in a fierce garlic-lemon-oregano marinade, potatoes roasted to glass-crisp in rosemary and thyme, broccoli charred until the tips frazzle — finished with a pan sauce made from the marinade itself.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min (+30 min–24h marinating)
  • 🔥 Cook: 40 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
640kcal
52gprotein
55gcarbs
20gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

Chicken & marinade

  • 2 × 200g chicken breasts, butterflied
  • 15ml olive oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 lemon — zest & all its juice, spent halves kept
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Salt & black pepper

Crispy herb potatoes

  • 500g baby new potatoes, halved
  • 15ml olive oil
  • 2 sprigs rosemary, needles chopped
  • 3 sprigs thyme, leaves picked
  • 1 tsp flaky salt

Broccoli

  • 200g Tenderstem broccoli
  • 5ml olive oil
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • Pinch chilli flakes

Equipment

  • Large baking tray
  • Griddle or heavy frying pan
  • Small saucepan (pan sauce)
  • Zester, garlic crusher

Method

  1. Marinate (up to 24h ahead). Whisk the oil, garlic, lemon zest and juice, oregano, mustard, honey, 1 tsp salt and plenty of pepper. Pour two-thirds over the butterflied chicken in a dish or bag (reserve the rest — it becomes the sauce). Minimum 30 minutes; overnight transforms it.
  2. Potatoes first. Oven to 210°C fan. Parboil the halved potatoes in well-salted water for 8 minutes; drain and shake hard in the colander until the edges scuff — scuffed edges are where crispness comes from. Toss with the oil, rosemary, thyme and flaky salt on the tray, cut-side down. Roast 30–35 minutes without touching them for the first 20.
  3. Broccoli in. When the potatoes have 12 minutes left, push them to one side and add the broccoli tossed with its oil, garlic and chilli flakes. It should come out with frazzled tips and a squeaky-tender stem.
  4. Chicken. Meanwhile, heat the dry griddle until smoking. Shake excess marinade off the chicken and cook 4–5 minutes per side, pressing gently, until charred in stripes and cooked through (74°C at the thickest point). Add the spent lemon halves cut-side down alongside for the final 2 minutes. Rest the chicken 4 minutes under foil.
  5. The 90-second pan sauce. Tip the reserved (untouched) marinade into a small saucepan with 3 tablespoons of water and any chicken resting juices. Boil hard for 90 seconds until glossy and slightly thickened.
  6. Serve. Potatoes and broccoli sharing the plate, chicken sliced thick on the diagonal, sauce spooned over the chicken, charred lemon halves for squeezing over absolutely everything.

Chef’s tip

Butterflying is the difference between juicy and dry: even thickness means the thin end doesn’t overcook while the thick end catches up. Open each breast like a book and flatten gently with your palm.

Make ahead

Marinate the night before — it’s better that way anyway. Potatoes can be parboiled and scuffed in the morning.

Storage

Leftover chicken keeps 3 days and makes a superb cold addition to the next day’s salad or pitta. Potatoes re-crisp in a 200°C oven, never a microwave.

Freezing

Raw chicken in marinade: 3 months, freezer-bag flat. Cooked components: fridge food.

For your daughter

Load her plate with 250–300g of the potatoes to your 200g — roast an extra handful; they vanish anyway — plus buttered corn or garlic bread if it’s been a training day.

Dinner 02 · Fish, the Italian way · 35 minutes

Baked Cod Puttanesca with Olives, Capers & Basil-Crushed New Potatoes

Thick flakes of cod baked in a punchy Neapolitan sauce of tomatoes, olives, capers and a whisper of anchovy — the sauce does all the shouting so the fish doesn’t have to. Served over new potatoes crushed with basil and olive oil.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 10 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 30 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
590kcal
45gprotein
52gcarbs
18gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 2 × 160g cod loins (thick cuts, not tail fillets)
  • 15ml olive oil
  • 1 small onion, finely diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 3 anchovy fillets (optional but strongly advised — they dissolve into savoury depth, not fishiness)
  • ¼ tsp chilli flakes
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 12 Kalamata olives, halved
  • 1 tbsp capers, rinsed
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • ½ tsp honey
  • 450g baby new potatoes
  • 10ml extra virgin olive oil (for the crush)
  • 15g basil leaves
  • Lemon wedges, to serve · salt & pepper

Equipment

  • Ovenproof frying pan or shallow casserole
  • Medium saucepan
  • Potato masher or fork

Method

  1. Oven to 190°C fan. Potatoes into cold salted water; boil 15–18 minutes until completely tender.
  2. Meanwhile, the sauce: oil into the ovenproof pan over medium heat. Onion with a pinch of salt, 5 minutes. Garlic, anchovies and chilli flakes for 2 minutes, mashing the anchovies until they vanish into the oil.
  3. Tomatoes, olives, capers, oregano and honey. Simmer 8–10 minutes until thick and brick-red. Taste — capers and olives bring salt, so season last and lightly.
  4. Season the cod, nestle the loins into the sauce, spoon a little over each, and slide the pan into the oven for 10–12 minutes — until the fish flakes into fat, opaque petals at the push of a fork. Thick loins need the full 12; check at 10.
  5. Drain the potatoes, return to the pan, and crush once each with the masher — broken, not mashed. Fold through the extra virgin oil, most of the basil (torn), salt and pepper.
  6. Basil-crushed potatoes in wide bowls, cod and a generous flood of sauce over, remaining basil and a lemon wedge on top.

Chef’s tip

Cod is done at the moment it turns from translucent to opaque — carry it one minute past and it tightens. When in doubt, pull it; residual sauce heat finishes the job on the plate.

Make ahead

The sauce (steps 2–3) keeps 3 days and freezes 3 months — with a batch banked, this becomes a 15-minute dinner.

Storage

Cooked, 24 hours in the fridge; eat the leftovers cold or gently warmed — reheated cod overcooks fast.

Freezing

Sauce: yes, 3 months. The finished dish with fish: no.

For your daughter

150g extra potatoes in the pan for her, crusty bread for the sauce, and if olives are contested territory, cluster them on your side of the pan before serving.

Dinner 03 · Batch hero · The Monday anchor

Rich Turkey Ragu with Wholemeal Spaghetti, Hidden Vegetables & Parmesan

A slow-tasting Bolognese-style ragu built on lean turkey and a secret second act of finely grated vegetables that melt into the sauce and make it silkier, sweeter and bigger. Nobody has ever detected the courgette. Nobody ever will.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 50 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 4 (eat 2, freeze 2)
630kcal
48gprotein
68gcarbs
14gfat
10gfibre

Ingredients (serves 4)

  • 15ml olive oil
  • 500g turkey mince (2% fat)
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • 2 carrots, finely grated
  • 1 courgette, finely grated & squeezed
  • 150g mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 2 × 400g tins chopped tomatoes
  • 200ml chicken stock
  • 1 tsp dried oregano + 1 bay leaf
  • 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • ½ tsp honey
  • 220g wholemeal spaghetti, to serve 2 tonight (90g him / 130g her — cook fresh pasta again for the freezer half)
  • 20g Parmesan, grated
  • Basil, to finish · salt & pepper

Equipment

  • Large casserole or deep sauté pan
  • Box grater
  • Large pasta pan

Method

  1. Heat the oil in the casserole over high heat. Brown the turkey hard in two batches, breaking it up and leaving it alone long enough to catch bronze at the edges — colour is flavour, and lean mince only gives it up if you stop stirring. Set aside.
  2. Drop to medium. Onion, carrot, courgette and mushrooms with a pinch of salt for 8–10 minutes, until the vegetable pile has collapsed to half its size and the pan is nearly dry.
  3. Garlic for 1 minute; tomato purée for 2, stirring until it turns brick-dark.
  4. Turkey back in, with the tomatoes, stock, oregano, bay, balsamic and honey. Simmer, lid ajar, 30–35 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thick enough to hold a wooden spoon’s trail. Add a splash of water if it tightens early.
  5. Cook the spaghetti in loudly salted water to 1 minute under packet time. Reserve a mug of pasta water.
  6. Toss half the ragu with the pasta and a splash of the starchy water until every strand is coated and glossy — the toss in the pan, not the ladle on top, is what makes it taste Italian. Parmesan, basil, black pepper. Freeze the other half of the ragu.

Chef’s tip

The balsamic-and-honey pair at the end of step 4 is the “simmered all day” cheat — acid and sweetness rounding the tomatoes in a way an extra hour otherwise would.

Make ahead

The ragu is better tomorrow and better still the day after. Make Sunday, eat Monday: the plan already knows.

Storage

Ragu: fridge, 3 days. Always store sauce and pasta separately; cook pasta fresh each time (it takes 10 minutes and is 90% of the eating quality).

Freezing

The ragu freezes for 3 months and is the backbone of the plan’s freezer rotation. Flat bags, labelled, defrosted overnight in the fridge.

For your daughter

Her spaghetti portion is 130g dry to your 90g, plus garlic bread when she asks (she will), and cheese without accountancy.

Dinner 04 · The underrated cut

Smoked Paprika Pork Tenderloin with Quick Romesco, Charred Peppers & Lemon Rice

Pork tenderloin is the leanest meat most people never cook — as lean as chicken breast, twice as interesting. Rubbed in smoked paprika and roasted pink-blushing perfect, over a five-minute romesco of roasted peppers and almonds that tastes like Barcelona.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 30 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (with romesco to spare)
645kcal
49gprotein
58gcarbs
20gfat
7gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

Pork

  • 1 × 400g pork tenderloin, trimmed of silverskin
  • 2 tsp sweet smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Salt & black pepper

Quick romesco

  • 200g roasted red peppers from a jar, drained
  • 30g whole almonds, toasted
  • 1 small garlic clove
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 2 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 10ml extra virgin olive oil
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika

Sides

  • 130g white basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ lemon, juice + zest
  • 2 peppers (any colour), quartered
  • 1 courgette, thick batons
  • 5ml olive oil
  • Parsley, chopped, to finish

Equipment

  • Ovenproof frying pan
  • Blender or stick blender
  • Baking tray · saucepan
  • Meat thermometer (worth owning for this cut)

Method

  1. Oven to 200°C fan. Rub the tenderloin with the paprika, oregano, garlic, oil, honey, 1 tsp salt and pepper. Rest at room temperature while the oven heats — cold-centre meat cooks unevenly.
  2. Peppers and courgette onto the tray with their oil and a pinch of salt; into the oven for 20–25 minutes until charred at the edges.
  3. Rice on: rinse, simmer in salted water 10–12 minutes, drain, fork through the lemon juice and zest, lid on to steam dry.
  4. Romesco: blitz all its ingredients with a pinch of salt to a coarse, rubbly cream — texture is the point; stop before smooth.
  5. Sear the tenderloin in the dry ovenproof pan over high heat, 2 minutes a side until burnished all round, then transfer the pan to the oven for 12–15 minutes — until the centre reads 63°C (or the faintest blush of pink remains). Rest 5 full minutes under foil; it climbs to 68°C and relaxes. This rest is not optional.
  6. Swipe romesco across each plate, lemon rice alongside, charred vegetables over, pork sliced into thick coins on top. Resting juices over the meat, parsley over everything.

Chef’s tip

Tenderloin’s only failure mode is overcooking — it has no fat to hide behind. Modern guidance (and every good restaurant) serves pork with a blush at 63°C rested. Grey pork is a choice, not a rule.

Make ahead

Romesco keeps a week in the fridge under a film of olive oil and improves by day two. Rub the pork in the morning.

Storage

Cooked pork: 3 days; superb cold and thin-sliced in the next day’s wrap or salad. Rice: 24 hours.

Freezing

Romesco freezes 3 months in small pots. Cooked tenderloin: fridge only — freezing dries it.

For your daughter

Cook 180g rice instead of 130g and hers is the bigger scoop, plus warm bread for the leftover romesco — which she will treat, correctly, as a dip.

Dinner 05 · One pan · Training-day dinner

One-Pan Spanish Chicken & Rice with Peppers, Peas, Olives & Saffron-Paprika Broth

Paella’s weeknight cousin: bronzed chicken thighs of flavour on breast-fillet macros, rice that drinks a smoky tomato broth in the same pan, sweet peppers, peas and olives. One pan, forty minutes, and the crusty bottom layer is legally the cook’s.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 12 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 38 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 generously
650kcal
48gprotein
66gcarbs
16gfat
7gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 350g chicken breast, large chunks (or 300g skinless thigh fillets)
  • 15ml olive oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 1 red + 1 yellow pepper, strips
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1½ tsp sweet smoked paprika
  • Pinch saffron (or ¼ tsp turmeric — honest substitute)
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 160g white basmati rice (dry)
  • 450ml hot chicken stock
  • 100g frozen peas
  • 10 pitted green or Kalamata olives, halved
  • 1 lemon, wedges
  • 2 tbsp flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • 28cm wide, shallow sauté pan with a lid (or wide casserole)
  • That’s it — one pan, as advertised

Method

  1. Season the chicken. Brown hard in the oil over high heat, 2–3 minutes per side — bronzed outside, still raw within. Remove.
  2. Medium heat: onion and peppers with a pinch of salt for 6–7 minutes until soft and sweet at the edges.
  3. Garlic, paprika and saffron for 45 seconds — spices burn fast, so keep moving. Purée in for 1 minute more.
  4. Rice in, stirred for a minute until every grain turns terracotta and glossy. Hot stock in — it will hiss theatrically. Season, level the rice, and nestle the chicken on top with its juices.
  5. Lid on, heat low. 18 minutes, no stirring, no peeking. Stirred rice turns gluey; abandoned rice turns perfect. Scatter the peas and olives over for the final 4 minutes (just lift the lid and drop them in).
  6. Off the heat, lid on, 5 minutes’ rest. Parsley, lemon wedges, and serve from the pan at the table. If the bottom layer has crisped — that’s socarrat, and it is the best part.

Chef’s tip

The no-stir rule is the whole recipe. Rice cooked in flavoured liquid, undisturbed, sets into distinct savoury grains; stirred, it becomes risotto’s sad cousin.

Make ahead

This one cooks fresh — but chop everything in the morning and it’s a 10-minute assembly plus unattended simmering.

Storage

24 hours (the rice rule, Chapter 06). Cool fast, fridge, reheat once until piping hot — or better, eat the leftovers cold as a Spanish rice salad with lemon.

Freezing

Possible (rice rules apply) but not the best use of the dish — it’s a cook-and-eat pan.

For your daughter

Cook 200g rice and 500ml stock in the same pan and portion her 60% of the rice; add crusty bread and aioli-style yogurt (yogurt + crushed garlic + lemon + olive oil) for dipping.

Dinner 06 · Traybake · Oily fish night

Harissa-Honey Salmon Traybake with Sweet Potato Wedges, Courgette & Lemon-Dill Yogurt

One tray, one bowl of sauce, zero drama: salmon fillets lacquered in harissa and honey, roasted over sweet potato wedges and courgette until the glaze catches at the edges. The cold lemon-dill yogurt against the warm spice is the whole event.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 12 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 35 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
660kcal
42gprotein
52gcarbs
28gfat
9gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 2 × 140g salmon fillets, skin on
  • 2 tsp harissa paste
  • 2 tsp honey
  • ½ lemon, juice (other half in wedges)
  • 500g sweet potatoes, skin-on wedges
  • 2 courgettes, thick diagonal slices
  • 1 red onion, wedges
  • 15ml olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 15g mixed seeds, toasted
  • Salt & black pepper

Lemon-dill yogurt

  • 150g 0% fat Greek yogurt
  • 1 tbsp fresh dill, chopped
  • ½ lemon, juice + zest
  • 1 small garlic clove, crushed · pinch of salt

Equipment

  • Large baking tray (the largest you own)
  • Small bowls for glaze & yogurt
  • Pastry brush (or a spoon and confidence)

Method

  1. Oven to 200°C fan. Toss the sweet potato wedges with 10ml of the oil, the cumin and salt. Spread out on the tray — crowded wedges steam; spaced wedges roast. 15 minutes alone first.
  2. Stir together the harissa, honey and lemon juice.
  3. After the wedges’ head start, add the courgettes and onion tossed in the last 5ml oil. Back in for 10 minutes.
  4. Make room in the centre of the tray for the salmon, skin-side down. Brush the fillets thickly with the harissa-honey glaze. Roast 10–12 minutes, until the salmon flakes at a nudge but the centre is still deep coral — and the glaze has gone sticky at the edges.
  5. Meanwhile stir the yogurt ingredients together. Cold sauce, warm tray: don’t skip it.
  6. Serve from the tray: wedges and vegetables, salmon on top, yogurt in fat spoonfuls, toasted seeds and lemon wedges over. Black pepper, table, done.

Chef’s tip

Salmon is cooked when a fork slides between the flakes with light pressure and the very centre still looks glossy — around 50°C inside. The ten extra degrees people usually add is where “salmon is dry” comes from.

Make ahead

Wedges can be cut and yogurt made the night before. The traybake itself is already the lazy option — lean into it.

Storage

Leftovers, 24 hours: the salmon and wedges are excellent cold with leaves and the yogurt as dressing. Don’t reheat the fish.

Freezing

Not suitable cooked. Buy the salmon frozen for economy; glaze from frozen-then-thawed works identically.

For your daughter

Add 200g more sweet potato wedges to the tray (they cost pennies and she’ll eat them all), and make her glaze half-harissa half-honey if she prefers gentler heat.

Dinner 07 · Saturday night · Steak, done right

Seared Sirloin with Chimichurri, Charred Peppers & Garlic-Crushed Baby Potatoes

Steak night survives the plan — because a trimmed sirloin is lean, and because chimichurri, the sharp green Argentine herb sauce, turns 220g of beef into an occasion. Hot pan, brown butter-less baste, proper rest. Saturday is safe.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 25 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
635kcal
50gprotein
48gcarbs
24gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

Steak

  • 2 × 220g sirloin steaks, fat rim trimmed, 2.5cm thick
  • 5ml olive oil
  • Flaky salt & coarse black pepper

Chimichurri

  • 25g flat-leaf parsley, chopped fine
  • 1 tbsp fresh oregano (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • ½ red chilli, minced
  • 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 20ml extra virgin olive oil
  • ½ tsp flaky salt

Sides

  • 450g baby potatoes
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 3 peppers (mixed colours), quartered
  • 100g green beans

Equipment

  • Cast-iron or heavy frying pan
  • Baking tray · saucepan
  • Tongs — never a fork
  • Warm plate for resting

Method

  1. Chimichurri first — it needs 20 minutes for the vinegar to marry the herbs. Stir everything together; it should look loose and vivid, not blended. Set aside at room temperature.
  2. Steaks out of the fridge 30 minutes before cooking. Oven to 210°C fan; peppers on a tray with a few drops of oil, roasted 20–25 minutes until collapsed and charred.
  3. Boil the potatoes in salted water 14–16 minutes until tender; drain, crush lightly, and fold with the crushed garlic, oil and salt. Keep warm. Beans into the potato water for the final 3 minutes.
  4. Pat the steaks completely dry, rub with the oil, season heavily on both sides. Get the pan just short of smoking — a drop of water should skitter and vanish.
  5. Steaks in: 2½ minutes per side untouched for medium-rare on a 2.5cm steak (add 60–90 seconds per side for medium). Press-test: soft yields rare, springy is medium. Tongs only — punctured steaks bleed out their advantage.
  6. Rest 5 full minutes on a warm plate — as long as the sear took. Slice against the grain, thick. Plate with the potatoes, peppers and beans; spoon chimichurri generously over the steak and let it run into everything else. Resting juices into the chimichurri, obviously.

Chef’s tip

Dry surface, hot pan, full rest: the holy trinity of steak. Of the three, the rest is the one home cooks skip — and it’s the one that keeps the juice in the meat instead of on the board.

Make ahead

Chimichurri holds 3 days in the fridge (it deepens); bring to room temperature before serving. Everything else is à la minute — that’s the point of steak night.

Storage

Leftover steak (a theoretical concept) keeps 3 days; slice thin and eat cold or flash-warmed in next day’s wrap with the leftover chimichurri.

Freezing

Raw steaks freeze well, tightly wrapped, 3 months. Chimichurri freezes in ice-cube trays. Cooked steak: don’t.

For your daughter

Same steak — she needs the iron more than anyone at the table. Her plate gets 300g of the potatoes to your 200g, plus buttered corn on the cob when it’s in season.

Dinner 08 · Batch hero · One pot

Greek Beef & Orzo One-Pot (Youvetsi) with Cinnamon, Tomato & Feta

Greece’s great taverna casserole: beef simmered with tomato, red wine vinegar, a stick of cinnamon and bay until the sauce goes glossy, then orzo cooked directly in the pot so it drinks everything. Feta over the top, straight to the table in the pan.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 1 hr 15 min (mostly unattended)
  • 🍽️ Serves: 4 (eat 2, freeze 2)
640kcal
47gprotein
62gcarbs
18gfat
7gfibre

Ingredients (serves 4)

  • 15ml olive oil
  • 700g lean diced beef (5% fat braising steak, trimmed)
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes + 250g passata
  • 600ml beef or chicken stock (plus more later)
  • 1 cinnamon stick (or ½ tsp ground)
  • 2 bay leaves · 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 250g orzo (dry)
  • 80g feta, crumbled
  • 2 tbsp parsley, chopped
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Large casserole with lid (hob-to-oven)
  • Wooden spoon

Method

  1. Oil into the casserole over high heat. Brown the beef in two batches, properly — dark crust, not grey sweat — 4–5 minutes per batch. Remove.
  2. Medium heat: onion and carrot with salt, 6–7 minutes. Garlic 1 minute, purée 1 more.
  3. Beef back in with the tomatoes, passata, stock, cinnamon, bay, oregano, vinegar and honey. Simmer, lid on, over the lowest heat (or 150°C fan oven) for 50–60 minutes, until the beef gives no resistance to a spoon.
  4. Fish out the cinnamon and bay. Stir in the orzo and 200ml more hot stock or water. Simmer, lid off, 10–12 minutes, stirring every couple of minutes as the orzo drinks and the pot turns creamy. It should be loose like risotto — add splashes of water as needed.
  5. Taste, season hard, and remember half of this pot is for the freezer — box it up now, before the feta (see notes).
  6. Feta and parsley over the remaining half; lid on for 2 minutes so the feta softens into snowdrifts. Serve in bowls with black pepper and, ideally, a Greek salad alongside.

Chef’s tip

The cinnamon stick is not a curiosity — it’s the signature of Greek beef dishes, warmth without sweetness. Trust it; nobody will name it, everybody will notice it.

Make ahead

Even better made through step 3 the day before; add the orzo when reheating for the freshest texture.

Storage

Fridge, 3 days. The orzo thickens dramatically as it sits — loosen with stock and re-season on reheat.

Freezing

Freeze the beef-and-sauce stage (before orzo) for best results, 3 months; cook orzo fresh into the defrosted stew. Cooked-with-orzo freezes acceptably if slightly undercooked first. Freeze without feta either way.

For your daughter

The deeper bowl, extra feta, and thick bread for wiping the pot — this is one of the dishes she’ll request into adulthood. Portion accordingly.

Dinner 09 · 20 minutes · Midweek rescue

Prawn, Chilli & Lemon Linguine with Courgette Ribbons, Garlic & Parsley

The great twenty-minute pasta: sweet prawns, a controlled burn of chilli, courgette ribbons that half-melt into the sauce, and an emulsion of lemon, olive oil and starchy pasta water that clings to every strand. Faster than a takeaway app.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 10 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 12 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2
600kcal
38gprotein
70gcarbs
16gfat
10gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2)

  • 300g raw king prawns, deveined, patted dry
  • 180g wholemeal linguine (80g him / 100g her)
  • 20ml olive oil, divided
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced thin as coins
  • 1 red chilli, deseeded, fine slices
  • 2 courgettes, ribboned with a peeler (stop at the seedy core)
  • 1 lemon — zest & juice
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 25g flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • Salt & black pepper

Equipment

  • Large pasta pan
  • Large frying pan
  • Vegetable peeler · zester
  • A mug (for the sacred pasta water)

Method

  1. Linguine into furiously boiling, well-salted water; cook to 2 minutes under packet time. Before draining, steal a full mug of the cooking water. The recipe does not work without it.
  2. While the pasta cooks: 15ml oil into the frying pan over high heat. Prawns in one layer, 90 seconds until blistered underneath, flip, 60 seconds. Out of the pan, barely done.
  3. Same pan, medium heat, remaining oil: garlic and chilli for 60–90 seconds until the garlic turns pale gold — one shade darker and it’s bitter, so pull early.
  4. Tomatoes in for 2 minutes until they slump; then the courgette ribbons for 1 minute — they should bend, not brown.
  5. Drained pasta into the frying pan with half the mug of pasta water, the lemon zest and juice. Toss violently over high heat for a minute — the water and oil emulsify into a light sauce that grips the pasta. Add more water if it tightens.
  6. Prawns back in with their juices for 30 seconds, then the parsley, a final glug-restraint of pepper, and serve in warm bowls immediately. Pasta waits for no one.

Chef’s tip

Cook the prawns twice-shy: barely done in step 2, finished for 30 seconds in step 6. Prawns that ride through the whole sauce build turn to rubber bands.

Make ahead

Ribbon the courgettes and slice the aromatics in the morning; the dinner itself is faster than any version of preparing it in advance.

Storage

24 hours at most, eaten cold as a pasta salad with extra lemon — reheated prawn pasta punishes everyone involved.

Freezing

No. But raw prawns from frozen are the standing recommendation, so the freezer already did its bit.

For your daughter

Her share of the pasta is 100g dry (already built into the ingredients), plus garlic bread if the day earned it. Deseed the chilli fully or split the pan before the chilli goes in — her half can take a gentler pinch.

Dinner 10 · Batch hero · The freezer’s crown jewel

Moroccan Chicken & Apricot Tagine with Chickpeas, Almonds & Herbed Couscous

A weeknight tagine that tastes like it took all afternoon: chicken braised with ras el hanout, cinnamon and honey until the sauce turns amber, apricots collapsing into sweetness, chickpeas holding the line. Over herbed couscous with toasted almonds — this is the dish guests refuse to believe is “on the plan”.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 15 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 45 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 4 (eat 2, freeze 2)
625kcal
46gprotein
60gcarbs
17gfat
9gfibre

Ingredients (serves 4)

Tagine

  • 15ml olive oil
  • 600g chicken — half breast chunks, half skinless thigh fillets (all breast works)
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp ras el hanout
  • 1 tsp ground cumin · ½ tsp cinnamon
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 350ml chicken stock
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 80g dried apricots, halved
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 1 lemon — ½ juiced, ½ in wedges
  • 20g flaked almonds, toasted
  • Coriander, big handful, chopped

Herbed couscous (per 2 servings)

  • 100g wholewheat couscous · 150ml hot stock
  • 1 tbsp each parsley & coriander, chopped
  • Squeeze of lemon

Equipment

  • Large casserole or deep sauté pan with lid
  • Heatproof bowl (couscous)

Method

  1. Brown the chicken in the oil over high heat, in batches, until golden at the edges. Remove. (Thigh pieces first — they take the heat better.)
  2. Medium heat: onions with a pinch of salt, 8 minutes, until soft and starting to gild. Garlic, ras el hanout, cumin and cinnamon for 1 fragrant minute; purée for 1 more.
  3. Chicken back in with the tomatoes, stock, chickpeas, apricots and honey. Simmer gently, lid ajar, 30 minutes, until the sauce is thick and amber and the apricots have half-melted into it.
  4. Finish with the lemon juice. Taste: it should balance on the point between sweet, spiced and sharp — adjust with more lemon (sharper) or a thread of honey (rounder).
  5. Couscous: stock over, plate on top, 8 minutes, fork through the herbs and lemon.
  6. Box half the tagine for the freezer. Serve the rest over couscous, showered with almonds, coriander and a lemon wedge.

Chef’s tip

Ras el hanout blends vary — some lean floral, some fiery. Bloom it in the onions (step 2) and smell it: if it’s shy, add another teaspoon. The tagine should perfume the kitchen.

Make ahead

Like all braises, better the next day. The plan cooks it Thursday and freezes half precisely because of this.

Storage

Fridge, 3 days. Couscous, 2 days — but fresh couscous takes 8 minutes, so just make it fresh.

Freezing

Outstanding — 3 months, portion tubs, sauce protects the chicken completely. Defrost overnight; reheat to a full simmer; brighten with fresh lemon and coriander.

For your daughter

Make 150g couscous and portion her the bigger half (roughly 90g cooked more than yours), add a warmed flatbread, and don’t fight her for the apricots.

Dinner 11 · Burger night, unrepentant

Greek-Style Turkey Burgers with Feta, Oven Chips, Yogurt Slaw & Burger Sauce That Isn’t Mayonnaise

Burger night stays. Juicy turkey patties threaded with feta, oregano and grated onion; proper chips from actual potatoes; a crunchy yogurt slaw; and a paprika-yogurt burger sauce nobody will clock as yogurt. Friday-night food with weekday numbers.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 20 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 35 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 2 (4 patties — freeze 2, or feed friends)
655kcal
47gprotein
62gcarbs
19gfat
9gfibre

Ingredients (serves 2 + 2 frozen patties)

Burgers

  • 500g turkey mince (2% fat)
  • 60g feta, crumbled small
  • ½ onion, grated & squeezed
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1½ tsp dried oregano
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 25g porridge oats, blitzed
  • ¾ tsp salt, black pepper
  • 5ml olive oil, for the pan

Chips & assembly

  • 600g white potatoes, cut into 1.5cm chips, skin on
  • 15ml olive oil · 1 tsp smoked paprika · flaky salt
  • 2 wholemeal buns, toasted
  • 1 large tomato, thick slices · Little Gem leaves

Slaw & sauce

  • 150g red cabbage + 1 carrot, shredded
  • 100g 0% fat Greek yogurt (half for slaw, half for sauce)
  • 1 tsp Dijon · 1 tsp red wine vinegar (slaw)
  • 1 tsp tomato purée · ½ tsp smoked paprika · ½ tsp honey (sauce)

Equipment

  • Large baking tray
  • Frying pan (or griddle)
  • Mixing bowls

Method

  1. Chips first. Oven to 210°C fan. Soak the cut chips in cold water 10 minutes (starch off = crisper), dry completely, toss with oil, paprika and salt. Single layer, 30–35 minutes, turned once, until deep gold with rattly edges.
  2. Mix all the burger ingredients and knead one full minute. Fry the tester teaspoon; adjust seasoning. Shape 4 patties slightly wider than the buns (they shrink), thumb-dimple the centres (they dome). Fridge 10 minutes. Freeze two, raw, between squares of baking paper.
  3. Stir the slaw: cabbage, carrot, half the yogurt, Dijon, vinegar, pinch of salt. Stir the sauce: remaining yogurt, tomato purée, paprika, honey.
  4. Pan over medium-high with the oil. Patties in: 4–5 minutes per side, pressing never, until burnished and cooked through (74°C — turkey is not a pink meat).
  5. Toast the buns cut-side down in the burger pan for the final minute. This step costs nothing and improves everything.
  6. Build: sauce on the base, lettuce, patty, tomato, more sauce, lid. Chips alongside, slaw in a bowl, and no cutlery for the burger — house rules.

Chef’s tip

The thumb dimple (step 2) is why restaurant burgers are flat and homemade ones are meatballs in a bun. Press a shallow well into each raw patty’s centre; it rises back level as it cooks.

Make ahead

Patties shape a day ahead (fridge) and the slaw crisps up made 2 hours early. Chips: fresh only.

Storage

Cooked patties 3 days; slaw 2 days; sauce 4 days. Leftover patty, chopped over a Greek salad, is a covert 13th lunch.

Freezing

Raw patties: 3 months, paper between. Cook from defrosted (overnight, fridge). Cooked patties freeze too — 2 months, reheat covered at 180°C.

For your daughter

Cheese on hers (a slice of proper cheddar melted over the patty in the pan’s last minute), the larger share of the chips, and a proper milkshake blitzed from milk, frozen banana and a spoon of honey. Burger night is burger night.

Dinner 12 · Sunday comfort · Batch hero

Golden Fish Pie with Cod, Salmon & Prawns under Cheddar-Crusted Sweet Potato Mash

The full British comfort classic, quietly re-engineered: three fishes in a lemony crème fraîche sauce (no roux, no flour anxiety — a cornflour slurry does it in one minute), under sweet potato mash with a thin, serious crust of mature cheddar. Make two: one for tonight, one for a future Wednesday that will thank you.

  • ⏱️ Prep: 25 min
  • 🔥 Cook: 45 min
  • 🍽️ Serves: 4 (two 2-person pies — bake one, freeze one)
620kcal
48gprotein
55gcarbs
20gfat
8gfibre

Ingredients (serves 4)

Filling

  • 280g cod loin, big chunks
  • 260g salmon fillet, skinned, big chunks
  • 200g raw king prawns
  • 80g smoked salmon trimmings, snipped (the smoky backbone)
  • 1 leek, washed & sliced
  • 10ml olive oil
  • 300ml semi-skimmed milk
  • 150ml half-fat crème fraîche
  • 2 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tbsp cornflour, slaked in 2 tbsp cold milk
  • 1 lemon — zest & ½ juice
  • 100g frozen peas
  • 2 tbsp each dill & parsley, chopped
  • Salt & black pepper

Topping

  • 900g sweet potatoes, peeled, chunks
  • 300g white potatoes, peeled, chunks (the structural minority)
  • 30ml milk · 10g butter
  • 60g mature cheddar, grated

Equipment

  • Two ~1L ovenproof dishes (or one 2L)
  • Large saucepan + masher
  • Medium saucepan (sauce)

Method

  1. Both potatoes into cold salted water; boil 15–18 minutes until tender. Drain, steam-dry 2 minutes in the colander (wet mash slides off a pie), then mash with the milk, butter, salt and pepper until smooth.
  2. Oven to 190°C fan. Soften the leek in the oil over medium heat, 5 minutes, without colour.
  3. Add the milk, crème fraîche and mustard; bring to a bare simmer. Stir in the cornflour slurry and cook 1 minute until it coats a spoon — a fish pie sauce in 60 seconds, no roux.
  4. Off the heat: lemon zest and juice, herbs, peas, ½ tsp salt, plenty of pepper. Fold in the raw cod, salmon, prawns and smoked salmon — raw, deliberately: they cook precisely once, in the oven, instead of twice into rubber.
  5. Divide between the two dishes. Mash on top starting from the edges (seals the coastline against eruptions), rough up the surface with a fork — ridges crisp — and snow the cheddar over. Freeze one pie now, unbaked.
  6. Bake the other 30–35 minutes until bronzed and bubbling at the borders. Rest 5 minutes. Serve with green beans or peas and a wedge of lemon.

Chef’s tip

The smoked salmon trimmings are the cheapest ingredient doing the most work — they smoke the entire sauce for about £1.50. Never skip them, never substitute more fresh salmon.

Make ahead

Assemble both pies (unbaked) up to 24 hours ahead, fridge, then bake plus 5 minutes. The Sunday-assembly, Monday-bake rhythm works beautifully.

Storage

Baked pie: fridge, 24 hours; reheat portions at 180°C for 20 minutes until piping hot right through. Once only.

Freezing

The unbaked pie freezes for 2 months. Defrost completely in the fridge (a full 24 hours), then bake as normal plus 10 minutes. Do not bake from frozen — the centre loses the race with the crust.

For your daughter

Cut the baked pie 60/40 in her favour — the mash-heavy corner is hers — with buttered peas and bread. Fish pie is how teenagers who “don’t like fish” discover they like fish.
