Chapter 05

Meal Prep Guide

Ninety minutes on Sunday, twenty on Wednesday. That is the entire time cost of never wondering what’s for dinner.

The philosophy: prep components, not plated meals

This is not the sad-Tupperware school of meal prep, where Sunday-you condemns Thursday-you to a dried-out chicken breast. Instead, you prepare components — marinated proteins, cooked grains, roasted vegetables, sauces — and assemble fresh in minutes. The food tastes cooked-today because most of it was finished today; only the slow, boring parts happened in advance.

Each week needs two sessions:

  • The Sunday Session (60–90 minutes) — covers Monday to Wednesday.
  • The Wednesday Top-Up (20 minutes) — covers Thursday to Friday. Do it while Wednesday’s dinner cooks.

The Sunday Session — the running order

The order matters: ovens and hobs run in parallel, hands stay busy, and you finish in one washing-up cycle. A typical week 1 session looks like this:

  1. Minute 0 — oven on, grains on. Heat the oven to 200°C fan. Get brown rice (for the week’s bowls) simmering — 350g dry, 25 minutes. Set a timer.
  2. Minutes 5–15 — marinate the proteins. Make this week’s marinades in freezer bags: souvlaki chicken (lemon, garlic, oregano, olive oil), harissa chicken. Add the chicken, squeeze out the air, fridge. Two days of flavour development for five minutes of work.
  3. Minutes 15–35 — roast a tray army. Fill two trays: one with peppers, courgettes and red onion (couscous lunches, sides), one with any potatoes or sweet potatoes the early-week meals need. Olive oil, salt, 25–30 minutes.
  4. Minutes 20–40 — make the sauces. While trays roast: one batch of tzatziki (it improves overnight), one jar of lemon-herb dressing (3 parts olive oil, 1 part lemon juice, Dijon, honey, salt — shake), and any spice mixes the week calls for.
  5. Minutes 40–60 — cook the batch recipe. Sunday is when the week’s ×2 recipe gets made — the turkey ragu, the youvetsi, the minestrone. It simmers unattended while you do everything else, then half goes into the freezer in labelled containers (see Chapter 06).
  6. Minutes 60–75 — breakfast infrastructure. Two jars of Bircher oats (Monday, Tuesday). Toast a jar of the oat-almond crunch topping. Boil 4 eggs for snacks and the Niçoise.
  7. Minutes 75–90 — portion and label. Rice cooled and boxed (fridge, max 24h — rice rules in Chapter 06), vegetables boxed, proteins labelled with the day they’ll be cooked. Wipe down. Done.

The golden rule of the session

Cook proteins as close to eating as possible; prep everything around the protein in advance. A marinated raw chicken breast cooked Tuesday night beats a cooked one reheated every time — except for the deliberate batch recipes (ragus, stews, koftas, fish pie), which genuinely improve with a day’s rest.

The Wednesday Top-Up (20 minutes)

While Wednesday’s dinner is in the oven:

  • Marinate Thursday and Friday’s proteins;
  • Cook a second small batch of grains if the week needs it (couscous takes five minutes — often nothing to do);
  • Refresh the salad drawer: wash leaves, make one more jar of dressing;
  • Two more Bircher jars if the week’s rhythm calls for them;
  • Move anything needed from freezer to fridge for the coming days (Thursday-night freezer meals in weeks 3, 4, 7 and 8).

Kit worth owning

  • 10–12 glass containers with clip lids in two sizes (700ml and 1.2L). Glass reheats better, doesn’t stain, and lets you see what’s in the fridge.
  • Digital kitchen scales — the plan’s only measuring instrument that matters.
  • Two large baking trays that actually fit your oven side by side.
  • A large (28cm+) ovenproof sauté pan with a lid — the Spanish chicken, youvetsi and tagine all live in it.
  • Freezer bags and a marker pen. An unlabelled freezer is a museum of regret.
  • 500ml jam jars for Bircher oats and dressings.

Involve the fifteen-year-old

The single highest-return move in this whole book: give her the Sunday session’s sauce station. Tzatziki, dressings, the pancake batter for Saturday. She learns to cook, the session halves in length, and the plan becomes something you do together rather than something done to her. Teenagers who help cook a meal reliably eat it — this is the closest thing food science has to a cheat code.
